HDB Build-To-Order (BTO): How the Application Process Works

The Build-To-Order (BTO) scheme is the main channel through which the
Housing & Development Board (HDB) sells new public housing flats in
Singapore. Under BTO, HDB only starts constructing a housing project once
there is sufficient demand from applicants, which is why BTO flats
typically take three to four years to complete from the time of
application to key collection.

HDB launches BTO exercises multiple times a year, usually in February,
May, August, and November, releasing flats across a mix of mature and
non-mature estates. Each launch specifies the towns, flat types (2-room
Flexi, 3-room, 4-room, 5-room, and occasionally 3Gen flats), and the
indicative price range for each project.

To apply, applicants submit an online application during a roughly
one-week application window. Eligibility depends on citizenship,
household composition (such as the Public Scheme for families, the
Fiance/Fiancee Scheme, or the Single Singapore Citizen Scheme for
buyers aged 35 and above), and income ceilings, which vary by flat type
and scheme.

After the application window closes, HDB conducts a ballot. Applicants
are assigned a queue number, and within each flat type, applicants from
under-represented groups (such as first-timer families) are given
priority under schemes like the First-Timer priority and, in selected
projects, quotas reserved for the Married Child Priority Scheme, which
helps young couples live near their parents.

Applicants who are invited to select a flat, based on their queue number
and the supply available, proceed to choose a specific unit, sign the
Agreement for Lease, and pay a down payment. Buyers then wait for
construction to complete before collecting their keys. Resale levies
apply to some second-time buyers who have previously enjoyed a housing
subsidy, and a Minimum Occupation Period of five years applies before an
owner can sell the flat on the open market.
