PUB and NEWater: Singapore's Fifth National Tap

PUB, Singapore's National Water Agency, manages the country's water supply
through what it calls the "Four National Taps": water from local
catchment, imported water, NEWater, and desalinated water. NEWater is
high-grade reclaimed water produced by treating used water through a
multi-stage purification process of microfiltration, reverse osmosis, and
ultraviolet disinfection, which removes bacteria, viruses, and other
contaminants beyond the standards required for drinking water.

NEWater is used mainly for non-potable purposes such as industrial
processes in wafer fabrication plants and other manufacturing that
requires ultra-clean water, as well as for indirect potable use, where a
small proportion of NEWater is blended into reservoirs during dry periods
before undergoing further natural filtration and conventional water
treatment. At present, NEWater is able to meet up to 40% of Singapore's
total water demand, and PUB has stated an ambition to raise this to meet
up to 55% of future water demand by 2060 as part of Singapore's push for
water self-sufficiency.

Singapore currently operates five NEWater plants: Bedok, Kranji, Ulu
Pandan, Changi, and Keppel Marina East, the last of which is co-located
with a desalination plant. This co-location allows the plant to draw from
either seawater or used water depending on weather conditions, a design
called a dual-mode plant that adds resilience during dry spells when
reservoir levels fall.

PUB frames water conservation as complementary to expanding supply,
running public campaigns to reduce per-capita household water consumption
and requiring water efficiency labelling on fittings such as taps and
showerheads, so that demand-side management and the Four National Taps
work together to secure Singapore's long-term water supply.
