MOH and MediSave: Saving for Healthcare Needs

MediSave is a national medical savings scheme, part of the Central
Provident Fund (CPF) system, that requires working Singapore Citizens and
Permanent Residents to set aside a portion of their income to pay for
future personal or approved family members' hospitalization expenses and
selected outpatient treatments. The Ministry of Health (MOH) sets the
policies on what MediSave can be used for, while the CPF Board administers
the individual MediSave accounts.

Contributions to MediSave come from the same overall CPF contribution
that employers and employees make each month, with the exact allocation
to the MediSave sub-account depending on the member's age; older workers
have a larger share of their CPF contributions directed to MediSave to
help build up savings for anticipated healthcare needs in later life.
Self-employed persons are also required to contribute to MediSave based
on their net trade income.

MediSave can be used to pay for hospitalization bills, day surgery, and a
defined list of outpatient treatments such as chemotherapy, kidney
dialysis, and vaccinations under the National Childhood Immunisation
Schedule. There are withdrawal limits for different types of treatment,
which are periodically revised by MOH, so that MediSave savings are
preserved for major healthcare needs rather than depleted on smaller
expenses better suited to cash or insurance.

MediSave works alongside MediShield Life, a basic health insurance plan
that covers large hospital bills and selected costly outpatient
treatments, and the Community Health Assist Scheme (CHAS), which
subsidizes outpatient care at participating general practitioner and
dental clinics. Together, MediSave, MediShield Life, and MediFund (which
provides a safety net for those who cannot afford care even after
government subsidies and insurance) make up the "3Ms" of Singapore's
healthcare financing system, sometimes described alongside CHAS as an
extended framework for keeping healthcare affordable.
