LTA Certificate of Entitlement (COE): Singapore's Vehicle Quota System

The Certificate of Entitlement (COE) is the mechanism the Land Transport
Authority (LTA) uses to control the total number of vehicles on
Singapore's roads under the Vehicle Quota System. Anyone who wants to
register a new vehicle in Singapore must first secure a COE for the
relevant vehicle category, and the vehicle cannot be registered without
one.

COEs are allocated through an open bidding exercise held twice a month.
LTA groups vehicles into five categories: Category A covers smaller and
less powerful cars, Category B covers larger and more powerful cars,
Category C covers goods vehicles and buses, Category D covers
motorcycles, and Category E is the open category, which can be used to
register any vehicle type except motorcycles. Bidders submit the maximum
price they are willing to pay, and all successful bidders in a category
pay the same price, known as the Quota Premium, which is set by the
lowest successful bid.

A COE is valid for 10 years from the date of vehicle registration. When a
COE expires, the vehicle owner can either deregister the vehicle or
renew the COE for another 5 or 10 years by paying a Prevailing Quota
Premium, which is based on the moving average of recent COE prices in the
same category.

The total COE quota available each period is derived from the number of
vehicles being deregistered, adjusted for the government's vehicle growth
rate targets, which have been set at or near zero for most categories in
recent years to manage road congestion and land use. Because the quota is
tight relative to demand, COE premiums have historically been a
significant part of the total cost of owning a car in Singapore, on top
of the vehicle's open market value, taxes, and registration fees.
