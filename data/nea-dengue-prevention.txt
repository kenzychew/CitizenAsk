NEA Dengue Prevention: The Fight Against Aedes Mosquitoes

The National Environment Agency (NEA) leads Singapore's dengue prevention
programme, which centers on controlling the population of the Aedes
aegypti mosquito, the primary vector that transmits dengue, chikungunya,
and Zika viruses in Singapore. Unlike many other mosquito species, Aedes
aegypti breeds in small pockets of stagnant clean water and is most
active for feeding during the day, particularly in the two hours after
sunrise and before sunset.

NEA's approach rests on the "Mozzie Wipeout" community programme, which
asks residents to perform the "5-step Mozzie Wipeout" in their own homes
weekly: changing water in vases, removing water from flowerpot plates,
covering water storage containers, clearing blockages in roof gutters,
and turning over water storage containers such as pails when not in use.
These five simple actions target the most common breeding habitats found
during NEA's own inspections of households.

NEA officers conduct regular inspections of homes and common areas,
particularly in areas identified as dengue clusters, where the number of
locally transmitted cases has crossed a threshold within a defined
radius and time window. Households found with mosquito breeding can be
issued warnings or fines under the Control of Vectors and Pesticides Act,
and repeat offenders face escalating penalties.

NEA also deploys Project Wolbachia, which releases male Wolbachia-carrying
Aedes aegypti mosquitoes that, when they mate with wild female mosquitoes,
produce eggs that do not hatch, suppressing the local mosquito population
over time without pesticides. This is combined with routine larviciding
of drains and other public spaces, and public dengue cluster maps that
let residents check whether they live near an active cluster and take
extra precautions such as using mosquito repellent.
